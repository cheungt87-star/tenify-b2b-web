## 1. Brand Purpose & Philosophy

### Why We Exist

Moving into a new home is one of the most stressful experiences in adult life. And when something goes wrong with a deposit, tenants are almost always at a disadvantage — no documentation, no proof, no power.

TFI exists to change that.

We give tenants an independent, professionally documented record of their property's condition before they move in — so they can move out with their deposit intact, and their dignity intact.

### Design Philosophy (NEW)

**TFI feels like a trusted friend walking you through the process.**

Our design should feel like you're sitting across the table from a person who genuinely wants to help — not interacting with a system. Every screen, every component, every interaction should answer an unspoken question: *"Can I trust this person/company?"*

Three principles guide our visual design:
1. **Photography first** — Real homes, real people, real moments. No abstract icons or generic illustrations doing the work that photographs should do.
2. **Light and warm** — Bright, optimistic, never clinical. You're moving toward something good (your new home), not filling out a tax form.
3. **Trust visible** — Verification badges, agent credentials, certified marks, and human faces make abstract guarantees concrete and real.

## 3. Brand Positioning

### Target Audiences

**Primary — Tenants (Customers)**
People about to move into a rented property. Often anxious, time-pressured, and unfamiliar with their rights. They need **confidence and clarity rooted in real trust** — not jargon, not complexity, not empty promises.

They want to know: *"Can I trust this? Will my deposit be protected? Who exactly is coming to my home?"*

**Secondary — TFI Certified Agents**
Freelancers building a flexible income through professional inspections. Mobile-first, working in the field. They value tools that make their work easier, faster, and more professional — and they care about recognition and earnings.

They want to know: *"Will I be treated as a professional? Can I earn a real income? Will my work be valued?"*

**Tertiary — TFI Admins**
Internal operators managing agents, bookings, and platform quality. They value control, visibility, and workflow consistency.

## 4. Core Visual Pillars

### Pillar 1: Photography (Primary Design Element)

Photography is **not decoration**. It is **load-bearing structure** in our design system.

**Every screen that can carry a photograph should carry one.** Not because it looks nice, but because it removes uncertainty and builds trust through visual recognition.

#### What Photography Does

- **Removes abstraction** — A photograph of an actual inspected room is more convincing than a checkbox or a green badge
- **Builds recognition** — Seeing the actual agent (face, name, rating) who will visit your home is fundamentally different from a generic icon
- **Creates emotional connection** — A photograph of someone's first day in their apartment is more powerful than copy alone
- **Signals authenticity** — Real images (not stock photos) signal that this is a real service, run by real people

#### What Photography Shows

- **Agent faces and names** — Clear, friendly, professional photographs. Not headshots; contextual moments (agent at a property, agent with tools, agent smiling)
- **Real properties** — Actual homes being inspected. Not designer show homes. Lived-in spaces with character.
- **Real moments** — A tenant unlocking their front door. An agent taking notes. A family celebrating in their new apartment. A tenant reading a report with relief.
- **Details that matter** — Door handles, floor conditions, light fixtures. Close-ups that show the inspection is thorough and real.
- **Diverse contexts** — Different apartment types, neighborhoods, people. The spaces and faces our users recognize as their own.

#### Photography Style Guide

- **Real light, not styled** — Natural daylight, morning light, lived-in lighting. Not professionally lit show homes.
- **Warmth and texture** — Wooden floors, soft furnishings, plants, family photos on shelves. These signal "home" immediately.
- **Authentic people** — Diverse ages, backgrounds, contexts. Candid expressions (not forced smiles). Real emotion.
- **Scale and intimacy mix** — Establish shot of a full room, then close details (door handle, crack in wall, new fixture). Both matter to the story.
- **Color palette warmth** — Images should feel warm and natural. Stone, sage, coral tones in real spaces complement the UI palette naturally.
- **Cropping matters** — Photographs are cropped to draw focus. Not generic wide shots; specific, intentional framings.

#### What to Avoid

- Generic corporate stock photography (three people around a laptop, handshakes, generic city skylines)
- Staged show-home interiors that no one actually lives in
- Cold, blue-tinted professional photography
- Overly bright, sterilized images
- Stock "diverse people" poses that feel constructed
- Adversarial imagery (courtrooms, conflict, lawyers)
- Empty rooms or minimalist spaces

### Pillar 2: Trust Made Visible

Trust can't be promised; it must be **shown**.

#### Trust Signals (Integrated Throughout)

**Agent Profile Photos & Names**
Every agent interaction is anchored by a real photograph and full name. Not an avatar, not initials. A face and a name.

**Verification & Certification Badges**
Clear, consistent visual indicators:
- ✓ Certified badge (green + checkmark)
- ★ Rating stars (1-5, with count visible)
- 📋 Verification marks showing vetting completion
- 🏡 Number of inspections completed

These are **integrated into layouts**, not hidden in corners.

**Credentials & Review Count**
Visible information:
- "Certified Agent since [date]"
- "158 inspections completed"
- "4.9/5 from 127 tenant reviews"

Not generic; specific and current.

**Clear Process Steps**
Each screen in the booking flow shows: "Step 2 of 4 — Choose your preferred date". Progress is visible. No hidden steps.

**Transparency in Numbers**
Commission amounts shown clearly. Inspection costs shown upfront. No surprises. Clarity builds trust.

### Pillar 3: The Human Element

Design choices that make it feel like a person is helping you, not a system processing you.

#### Real Names & Faces Everywhere

- Agent introductions use full names + photos, not "Inspector #247"
- Tenant-facing messages are signed by real people ("From [Agent Name]")
- Error messages are written by someone who cares: "This photo needs better lighting — try the sunlit room instead" (not "Invalid image specification")

#### Warmth in Copy & Spacing

- Generous white space (not cramped forms)
- Warm backgrounds (Warm Stone #F6F4F0, not clinical white)
- Conversational language (no corporate jargon)
- Reassuring language at key moments ("You're almost done. Just a few more photos and we're finished.")

#### Progress & Celebration

- Show progress visually (segments filling as work completes)
- Celebrate milestones ("You've completed 3 inspections — you're a TFI pro!")
- Affirming language at completion ("Done. Report on its way to the tenant.")

## 5. Color System

### Color Philosophy (REFINED)

Our palette is rooted in **warmth, homes, and human advocacy** — not technology.

**Charcoal** anchors us with quiet authority and trust (see: trust signals).
**Sage** communicates calm, growth, and confirmed completion (see: protection without coldness).
**Coral** brings human energy and warmth — it's the color of real spaces and genuine moments.

**None of these colors look like a generic SaaS product, and that's deliberate.**

Color is **one layer** of our identity. It works in service of **photography, layout, and typography** — never as a substitute for them.

### Primary Palette

| Name | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TFI Charcoal** | `#2A3030` | 42, 48, 48 | Trust signals, headers, verified badges, agent names, navigation. Authority without coldness. |
| **TFI Sage** | `#6B9E7A` | 107, 158, 122 | Confirmed/completed states, progress fills, active indicators. Calm growth. |
| **TFI Coral** | `#D4785A` | 212, 120, 90 | Primary CTAs, highlights, human warmth moments. The energy of the brand. |

### Extended Warm Palette

| Name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm Stone** | `#F6F4F0` | Primary background for all tenant-facing pages — warm, paper-like, never stark white. All cards on tenant surfaces. |
| **Stone Mist** | `#EFECEA` | Slightly deeper surface for secondary cards, form fields, subtle contrast. |
| **True White** | `#FFFFFF` | Modals, overlays, agent app backgrounds (more functional). Use sparingly on tenant surfaces. |

### Neutral Palette (for text & structure)

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `neutral-900` | `#1A1C1C` | Body text, primary headings — warmth-compatible dark |
| `neutral-800` | `#323838` | Secondary text, metadata |
| `neutral-600` | `#6A7070` | Muted labels, placeholders, help text |
| `neutral-400` | `#B0B8B8` | Dividers, disabled states, secondary UI |
| `neutral-200` | `#E8ECEC` | Input backgrounds, subtle cards |
| `neutral-100` | `#F4F6F6` | App screen backgrounds, subtle fills |

### Semantic Colors (Trust & Action)

| Token | Hex | Meaning | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| `success` | `#5A8F68` | Completed, verified, confirmed | Checkmarks, completion badges, positive confirmations |
| `warning` | `#D4785A` | Action needed, partial progress | Alerts, pending items (same as coral) |
| `error` | `#C84040` | Validation failure, blocked action | Only for true errors, not warnings |
| `info` | `#2B7BE5` | System messages, tooltips | Helpful information (agent tips, how-to) |

### Color Accessibility

All primary text/background combinations meet **WCAG AA** (4.5:1) minimum.

**Key passing pairs:**
- `neutral-900` on `Warm Stone` ✓
- `neutral-900` on `True White` ✓
- `white` on `Charcoal` ✓
- `white` on `success green` ✓
- `Charcoal` text on `Warm Stone` ✓

**Important:** TFI Sage `#6B9E7A` does NOT meet AA for body text on light. Use for icons, fills, and decorative elements only.

### Light Theme (Only)

**TFI operates in light theme.** The entire design system is built for bright, daylight-clear interfaces.

Warm Stone (#F6F4F0) and pale neutrals create a paper-like, optimistic feel. Combined with photography and warm copy, this removes any clinical coldness.

## 7. Photography & Imagery

This section is **foundational** to TFI's visual identity.

### Imagery as Structure (Not Decoration)

Photography appears on:
- **Hero & onboarding screens** — Full-bleed or dominant image. Show a real front door, a real room, a real moment.
- **Agent introductions** — Photograph, full name, rating, review count. A real person, not an avatar.
- **Booking confirmation** — Paired with confirmation text: image of the agent or a welcoming interior. Removes uncertainty visually.
- **Empty states** — Illustrated or photographic content showing what could be here (a photo of an inspected property, not a generic icon)
- **Report delivery** — Show something tangible: a real inspection moment, the document being delivered, a completed property. Not a generic checkmark on a colored block.
- **Agent booking flow** — Property photos, location images, agent profile photos. Make the workflow feel real and grounded.

### Photography Style

#### Subject Matter
- **Real homes, real light** — Natural daylight, lived-in spaces. Not staged show-home photography. Tenants should recognize these spaces as places people actually live.
- **Warmth and texture** — Wooden floors, soft furnishings, plants, sunlight. These signal "home" immediately.
- **Real people, candid moments** — Diverse backgrounds, age ranges, contexts. An agent photographing a room. A tenant unlocking a door. A family's first moment in their new apartment.
- **Scale and intimacy** — Mix wide establishing shots (a full room, a street view of a building) with close details (a door handle, a light fixture, a newly painted wall). Both angles matter to inspection narratives.
- **Warmth in post-processing** — Images should feel warm and natural. Avoid heavy filters. Stone, sage, and coral tones in real spaces should complement the UI palette naturally.

#### Specific Contexts

**Agent Profile Photos**
- Friendly, approachable facial expressions
- Agent in context (at a property, with tools, in their car)
- Clothing that reflects professional-casual (not corporate, not overly casual)
- Natural lighting, outdoors where possible
- Clear face visibility (not shadowed or obscured)

**Property Photos (Inspected Spaces)**
- Various room types (bedrooms, kitchens, bathrooms, living areas)
- Showing the actual condition (not always pristine; real wear is okay)
- Good natural light (or honest artificial light)
- Wide shot to establish the space, then details
- Include architectural features (ceiling height, light switches, door condition)

**Tenant Moments**
- Genuine expressions (smiling, relieved, confident, calm)
- Real-life contexts (moving boxes, reading a report, signing documents)
- Diverse ethnicities, ages, family structures
- Moments that show the emotional journey (anxiety → confidence)

**Agent at Work**
- Taking photos of condition issues
- Making notes on the form
- Measuring or inspecting details
- Interacting with tenant or property manager
- Professional but not overly formal

### Illustration (When Photography Isn't Possible)

Where full photography isn't practical (error states, onboarding educationals, specific scenarios):

- **Line-based with warmth** — Thin strokes, rounded forms, filled with brand palette colors (charcoal, sage, coral)
- **Domestic subjects** — Front doors, keys, windows, clipboards, documents, floor plans, inspection tools
- **No SaaS illustration tropes** — No floating dashboards, no disembodied hands, no abstract globe graphics

### What to Avoid Absolutely

- Generic corporate stock photography (people around a laptop, awkward handshakes, bland city skylines)
- Adversarial imagery (courtrooms, lawyers, conflict)
- Cold, blue-tinted sterile interiors
- Show-home interiors (too perfect, no personality)
- Empty, minimalist spaces with no character
- Stock "diversity" images that look constructed or posed
- Oversaturated color grading
- Overly bright/harsh lighting

## 8. Spacing & Layout

### Spacing Scale (4px base grid)

```
space-1:  4px    — micro gaps, icon padding
space-2:  8px    — tight internal spacing
space-3:  12px   — compact list items
space-4:  16px   — standard component padding
space-5:  20px   — section internal spacing
space-6:  24px   — card padding, agent info blocks
space-8:  32px   — section gaps
space-10: 40px   — major vertical rhythm
space-12: 48px   — screen-level top padding
space-16: 64px   — hero/onboarding spacing
```

### Mobile Layout

- **Safe area**: Apply `env(safe-area-inset-*)` to all screen containers
- **Touch targets**: Minimum 44×44px (interactive) / 48×48px (primary actions)
- **Bottom navigation**: Fixed, 60px height + safe-area-bottom
- **Header**: Fixed, 56px height + safe-area-top
- **Content area**: Full bleed between header and nav, scrollable

### Web Layout

- **Hero sections**: Full-bleed photography (height: 400-500px, or taller on hero pages)
- **Content width**: Max 800px for body text (legibility), full width for hero images
- **Agent cards**: 3-4 column grid on desktop, 2 columns on tablet, 1 on mobile
- **Generous margins**: Large white space around content, especially on tenant-facing pages

### Border Radius

```
radius-sm:  4px    — chips, tags, small UI elements
radius-md:  8px    — cards, buttons, inputs, default
radius-lg:  12px   — sheets, modals, cards
radius-xl:  16px   — bottom sheets
radius-full: 9999px — pills, avatar badges
```

**Tenant surfaces use rounder corners** (lg/xl) for warmth.
**Agent surfaces may use tighter radii** (md) for efficiency where appropriate.

## 9. Brand Voice & Messaging

### Voice Principles

**Human first**
Every person using TFI is a real person in a real situation. Write for them, not for a style guide. An anxious tenant. A professional trying to earn. Use the tone of someone who understands.

**On their side**
TFI is an advocate. Language should feel like a trusted friend explaining something, not a corporation covering its bases. Avoid legal-ese, passive voice, and corporate filler.

**Warm but direct**
Warmth doesn't mean meandering. Get to the point — then wrap it in care.

**Specific and honest**
"Your agent Sarah will arrive at 2 PM on Tuesday" not "An agent will arrive soon."
"Inspection complete. You earned £45 commission" not "Payment has been processed."

**Empowering, not patronizing**
Tenants know their situation. Agents know their job. Give them clarity and confidence, not instructions for the obvious.

### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing / Landing** | Warm, confident, human | "Your deposit deserves protection. We're in your corner." |
| **Tenant booking flow** | Clear, reassuring | "Choose your preferred date. Sarah is available Tuesday–Thursday." |
| **Agent introduction** | Professional warmth | "Meet Sarah. 4.8★ from 89 inspections. She'll be thorough and respectful." |
| **Booking confirmation (tenant)** | Affirming, caring | "Perfect. Sarah will be at your property Tuesday at 2 PM. We'll send a reminder." |
| **Booking confirmation (agent)** | Motivating | "Booking claimed. Head to 42 Rosewood Lane — Sarah's inspection is at 2 PM." |
| **Report delivery** | Warm, proud | "Your inspection report is ready. You now have everything you need." |
| **Commission earned** | Celebratory | "Inspection complete! You earned £45 commission. It's been added to your account." |
| **Validation errors** | Clear, non-blaming | "This photo needs better lighting — try the bright room instead." |
| **Success moments** | Brief, affirming | "Done. Report on its way." |

### Key Messaging

**For Tenants**
"Move in with confidence. TFI sends a certified agent to document your property's condition before you unpack a single box — so your deposit is protected from day one. You'll see who's inspecting, exactly what they're checking, and proof that matters."

**For Agents**
"Turn your time into real income. TFI certifies you, sends you bookings, and pays you commission for every inspection you complete. Professional tools. Transparent earnings. Flexible hours. The earning opportunity you've been looking for."

**For Stakeholders**
"TFI is the first pre-move-in inspection platform built entirely for tenants — connecting them with certified independent agents who document property condition, protect deposits, and create a fairer rental market."

### Vocabulary

| Preferred | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Tenant / Customer | User, renter, occupant |
| Agent / Inspection professional | Inspector, surveyor, worker, operative |
| Booking | Job, gig, task, appointment |
| Commission / Earnings | Payout, reward, payment |
| Certification / Certified | Training, approval, verification |
| Assessment / Inspection | Survey, evaluation |
| Claim (a booking) | Accept, take, grab, book |
| Complete / Completed | Done, finished |
| Report | Document, file, assessment |

### Writing Rules

- **Active voice**: "Sarah will document the property" not "The property will be documented"
- **Sentence case**: All UI labels, buttons, headings (not Title Case)
- **First person plural on tenant-facing**: "We'll document everything" not "TFI will document"
- **Second person for direct address**: "Your report", "Your booking", "Your earnings"
- **No exclamation marks in functional UI** — reserve for genuine celebration (commission earned, first booking)
- **Numbers as numerals**: "3 items remaining" not "three items remaining"
- **Dates in format**: DD MMM YYYY (e.g., "14 Apr 2026")
- **No corporate filler**: "leverage", "synergy", "seamless", "solution"
- **Empathy over brevity**: It's okay to add one warm sentence if it builds trust

## 10. UI Application Guidelines

### Surface Differentiation

| Surface | Context | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenant-facing (web)** | Booking flow, landing pages, reports | Warm Stone background, rounder corners, generous spacing, photography prominent |
| **Tenant mobile app** | Booking, tracking, report viewing | Warm Stone background, human-centric, progress visible |
| **Agent mobile app** | Inspection workflow, bookings, earnings | Lighter background, information-dense, efficient, tools prominent |
| **Admin portal** | Internal operations | Neutral surfaces, high density, functional |

**Tenant surfaces feel warmer, roomier, more personal.** Agent surfaces feel professional and efficient. Admin surfaces feel functional and clear.

### Component Hierarchy

#### Buttons

| Type | Usage | Style |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Main action per screen | TFI Coral fill, white text, radius-md, 48px height |
| **Secondary** | Supporting actions | Charcoal outline, Charcoal text, radius-md |
| **Ghost** | Tertiary / lower priority | Transparent, neutral-600 text |
| **Danger** | Irreversible actions | Error red fill, white text |

**Tenant button copy is warmer**: "Book my inspection" (not "Submit booking").
**Agent button copy is action-oriented**: "Claim booking", "Submit photos", "Complete inspection".

Agent introductions are **structured for trust**:

## 11. Trust Design Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Agent Introduction

**Purpose:** Tenant meets their inspector before they visit. Trust is established through visibility.

**Elements:**
- Large agent photograph (real, friendly, contextual)
- Full name, not ID
- Certification badge with date
- Star rating + review count
- Number of inspections completed
- Real tenant review quote
- Availability window
- Option to message/call

**Example location:** Booking confirmation screen, first touchpoint after booking

### Pattern 2: Verification Cascade

**Purpose:** Each step of the inspection process shows completion and verification.

**Visual progression:**
1. "Booking confirmed" → ✓ Charcoal checkmark + agent name
2. "Agent acknowledged" → ✓ Checkmark + "Sarah accepted at 3 PM"
3. "Inspection in progress" → ⏱ "Sarah is at your property now"
4. "Inspection complete" → ✓ "Sarah submitted photos and ratings"
5. "Report ready" → ✓ "Your report is ready to view"

**Each step is time-stamped and specific.**

### Pattern 3: Real Moments in Empty States

**Purpose:** Instead of generic "No items yet" message, show a moment that explains what will appear.

**Examples:**
- "Awaiting your first booking" → Photo of an agent reviewing a property
- "No reports yet" → Photo of a tenant reading their first inspection report
- "Inspection in progress" → Photo of an agent taking photos of a room

### Pattern 4: Transparent Information Design

**Purpose:** Commission amounts, dates, property details—no surprises, ever.

**Principle:** If it's visible, it's trustworthy. If it's hidden, it's suspicious.

**Application:**
- Commission breakdown: "£45 base commission + £5 bonus for speedy completion = £50 total"
- Inspection cost: "£50 (paid by tenant, no hidden fees)"
- Timeline: "Inspection takes ~1 hour. Report delivered within 24 hours."

### Pattern 5: Real Names & Faces Across UI

**Purpose:** Humanize every interaction.

**Application:**
- Error messages signed: "Need better lighting? — Try the bright room. — Sarah"
- Confirmation messages from: "From your TFI team"
- Notifications: "Sarah Mitchell is 10 minutes away"
- Help content: "Tips from our agents" → real agent names + photos

## 12. Brand Protection

### Usage Rules

**Permitted**
- Use TFI wordmark and icon in approved sizes
- Reproduce brand colors using exact hex values
- Use Inter typeface from Google Fonts
- Include agent names, real photographs, and verification marks prominently
- Use warm, human language in all tenant-facing copy
- Integrate photography as primary design element

**Not Permitted**
- Stretch, rotate, or apply effects to logo
- Use brand colors in wrong proportions
- Generic agent avatars instead of real photos
- Corporate, cold, or clinical language in tenant flows
- Hidden commissions or fees
- Stock photography instead of real homes/people
- Removing agent names, faces, or ratings
- Abstract design without photography foundation

### Tone Compliance

Brand guardians should review all copy:

**For tenant-facing:**
> "Would a tenant feel supported and reassured reading this — or processed?"

If the latter, rewrite it.

**For agent-facing:**
> "Does this treat the agent as a professional earning real income — or as an interchangeable worker?"

If the latter, rewrite it.

### Asset Management

All brand assets (photography library, logo files, color swatches, typography files, icon sets) must be stored in a single source-of-truth library. Updates to guidelines must be versioned and communicated before implementation.

### Monitoring

- Review all tenant-facing UI output **quarterly** for brand compliance
- Audit external communications (email, PDF reports) against voice guidelines
- Check photography selection for authenticity and warmth
- Flag deviations by severity:
  - **Minor** (spacing, subtle color variance) → correct in next sprint
  - **Major** (missing agent photo, corporate language, generic stock photo) → correct immediately
